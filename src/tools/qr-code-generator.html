How to create a QR code

  1. Choose what the QR code should do: open a website, show text, join Wi-Fi, send an email, call or text a number.
  2. Fill in the details. The preview updates as you type.
  3. Optionally change the colors and size. Keep the foreground dark and the background light so every phone can scan it.
  4. Download as PNG for screens and documents, or SVG for print, since it scales to any size without blurring.

Do these QR codes expire?

Never. These are static QR codes: the information is stored directly in the pattern, not behind a redirect link. Many "free" QR generators create dynamic codes that stop working when a trial ends. Codes made here work forever, with no account and no tracking.

Tips for QR codes that scan reliably

  • Print at least 2 × 2 cm (0.8 × 0.8 in). Bigger is better for scanning from a distance.
  • Leave a blank margin around the code. The download includes one already.
  • Use High error correction if the code may get scratched, dirty or partly covered.
  • Always test-scan with your phone camera before printing hundreds of copies.

Frequently asked questions

How does the Wi-Fi QR code work?

When scanned with an iPhone or Android camera, it offers to join the network automatically, so guests don't need to type the password. It's ideal for cafés, offices and Airbnb hosts.

Can I use these QR codes commercially?

Yes. Use them on business cards, packaging, menus, flyers or anywhere else, free of charge.

Why won't my QR code scan?

The most common causes are low contrast (light foreground or dark background), printing too small, or a missing white border. Long text also creates dense codes, so shorten long URLs.
