What makes a password strong?

Password strength comes from length and randomness. Each extra character multiplies the number of possible passwords an attacker would have to try. A random 16-character password with letters, numbers and symbols would take longer than the age of the universe to guess, even for powerful computers.

The strength meter above shows entropy in bits, a standard measure of unpredictability:

Password safety tips

  • Use a different password for every account. When one site is breached, attackers try the same password everywhere else.
  • Use a password manager such as Bitwarden, 1Password or your browser's built-in manager, so you only remember one master password.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) for email, banking and social media.
  • Don't use personal details like names, birthdays or pet names. They're the first thing attackers guess.

Frequently asked questions

Is this password generator safe?

Yes. Passwords are generated on your device using crypto.getRandomValues, the browser's cryptographically secure random number generator. They are never sent over the internet or stored anywhere.

How long should my password be?

At least 12 characters for everyday accounts, and 16 or more for email, banking and your password manager's master password.

Why avoid look-alike characters?

Characters like 0 and O, or 1, l and I, are easy to confuse when reading a password aloud or typing it from paper. Excluding them slightly reduces strength, so add a character or two to compensate.
