How to compress an image

    1. Drop one or more JPG, PNG or WebP images into the box above, or click it to pick files.
    2. Adjust the quality slider. 70–80% is the sweet spot for most photos: much smaller files with no visible difference.
    3. Optionally pick a different output format or a maximum width to shrink large photos further.
    4. Click Download next to each image, or Download all.

    Why compress images?

    Large images slow down websites, fill up email attachments and eat into storage. A photo straight from a modern phone is often 3–8 MB, while a compressed version that looks identical on screen can be 300–800 KB. Smaller images mean:

    • Faster websites — page speed is a Google ranking factor and keeps visitors from leaving.
    • Easier sharing — stay under email and upload size limits for forms, job portals and social media.
    • Less storage — fit more photos on your phone, cloud drive or server.

    Which format should I choose?

    FormatBest forNotes
    JPGPhotosUniversally supported. No transparency.
    WebPWebsites, photos and graphicsUsually 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same quality. Supports transparency.
    PNGScreenshots, logos, graphics with textLossless, so quality settings don't reduce PNG size. Convert to WebP for big savings.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my images uploaded to a server?

    No. Compression happens entirely inside your browser using the HTML5 canvas. Your images never leave your device, which also makes this tool fast and safe for private photos.

    Why is my compressed PNG not smaller?

    PNG is a lossless format, so the quality slider has no effect on it. Choose WebP (keeps transparency) or JPG as the output format to dramatically shrink PNG files.

    Will compression remove photo metadata?

    Yes. Re-encoding strips EXIF metadata such as camera model and GPS location, which is good for privacy when sharing photos online.

    Is there a file size or number limit?

    There are no limits set by us. Very large images (50+ megapixels) depend on your device's memory, but typical photos compress in under a second.
